Some backstory on this:

I went cycling this morning, got a little lost, and I had my bike computer route me home. It took me on a new path that I haven't been on before -- a lovely gravel, double-track, multi-use path. It was shaded, quiet, and such a wonderful respite from the hot asphalt and cars. I absolutely loved it. But it had a major downside: It was only a few miles long, and both ends of it ended at private property lines with metal barriers and big "No Trespassing" signs nailed onto trees. However, it crossed paved roads, which is how I was able to get onto the trail.

As I turned around on the trail to find a way to jump back onto a nearby public road, I grumbled to myself about this situation. I thought it was likely that the county approached the property owners at the end of the trail to buy part of their land to keep the trail going, but the property owners were NIMBYs and wouldn't oblige. So the trail stays in its short, less-than-useful form all because two people wouldn't work with the county.

When I got home, I looked up the trail and found that it is called the [Iron Belle Trail](https://midnr.maps.arcgis.com/apps/webappviewer/index.html?id=981d6168f3b5400f8de1b69d1d674d10) here in Michigan. The trail supposedly goes along the north and south coasts of the upper peninsula starting in Ironwood on the border with Wisconsin, and it goes all the way down to Belle Isle in Detroit. It's an incredibly long trail. 

But if you look at the legend of the linked map, you'll also see that much of the trail is in the "proposed" state. The private property line that I encountered is one of those borders of actual trail and "proposed" trail. 

So I want to make part of this "proposed" trail go all the way to the next section of actual trail. It's only about ten more miles of trail to add. But there are a lot of unknowns. Why is the trail unfinished? Is my guess correct about people not wanting to sell part of their property? And how can I best get all of this information?

The unfortunate answer is that I have to do a lot of digging. When I searched for this trail, I ended up on Michigan's DNR website. The DNR states that there are at least a dozen different groups that work together in order to make this trail, but it doesn't tell me which group I should be working with.

Okay. The next step is likely to contact the DNR to figure that information out. But that's slow and going to take a while. I want to go fast.

What if I went to a meeting with the county's board of commissioners? Would they even know what I'm talking about? Who am I supposed to talk to about this? This also doesn't satiate my need for speed.

Realistically, the fastest way to do this research is through a search engine. If Google were to index meeting minutes from all of these different groups, I could likely figure out what exactly is blocking the trail from being able to be pushed forward. Who knows - maybe they ran out of money and they didn't even offer anything to the guy at the end of the trail yet because of it. 

Maybe there's something that I would be able to help with. Maybe they have a fund that I could donate to. Maybe I could volunteer to actually make the trail. Maybe they just need managerial leadership. Whatever it is, I'm willing to help because I want this trail to be connected. 

I'm likely not alone in thinking this, either. People generally want to make their communities a better place, but there's too much friction in them figuring out what they ought to do in order to help. Having easily searchable meeting minutes would alleviate this friction. Another easier option would be for the DNR to actually point me to which organization is responsible for the portion of the trail near my home. They could have put up a sign at the end of the trail with contact information as well.

The point I'm trying to make is that there are a lot of simple things that governments could do to minimize friction for people who want to advocate for improvements like this. I guess I will need to keep taking the hard path and hope that my cold-call emails to the DNR will get answered in a timely manner.
